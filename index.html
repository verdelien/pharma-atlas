Method

How these 100 companies were chosen and ranked, and which numbers still need verification.

Ranking basis

The ranking basis is FY2025 pharmaceutical revenue, counting prescription drug businesses only. For example, Johnson & Johnson counts only Innovative Medicine, Roche only the Pharmaceuticals division, Bayer only Pharmaceuticals, and Merck KGaA only Healthcare. Most companies have fiscal years ending December 2025; Japanese and Indian companies mostly have March fiscal years, and CSL and Aspen have June fiscal years, noted line by line in the table. Non-USD figures are converted at 2025 average exchange rates.

Ranking year: FY2025, data as of 2026-09-28.

Sources

  • #1–50 base: Drug Discovery & Development, Pharma 50 2026 (published 2026-03-31). The list ranks by drug business revenue, excludes non-drug segments, and converts at IRS annual average exchange rates.
  • Cross-check: Pharmaceutical Executive, Pharm Exec 50 2026 (published 2026-08, data from Evaluate). The full text is paywalled, so it is used here only to check the ordering at the top; both put Eli Lilly first.
  • #51–100 extension: company annual reports and results announcements (BeOne, BioMarin, BioNTech and Gedeon Richter verified in this round), with ChemAnalyst Top 100 Pharmaceutical Companies 2026 as a secondary reference. ChemAnalyst has duplicate entries and mixes total revenue with drug revenue for some companies; any figure inconsistent with a company's actual size was discarded.

Adjustments to Pharma 50

  • Perrigo removed: the company has divested its prescription drug business and is now a consumer self-care company.
  • Omitted companies added: BeOne, argenx, Octapharma, Fosun Pharma, Genmab, Alnylam, Pierre Fabre, CSPC, Aurobindo, Hikma. Their FY2025 revenue is above the Pharma 50 cut-off for No. 50 ($3.32B), but the original list did not include them.

Exclusions

The following kinds of company do not enter the core 100. If they matter for understanding the collaboration history of a drug, they appear in the graph as supporting nodes.

  • Pharma distribution: the distribution parts of Sinopharm and China Resources Pharmaceutical
  • Animal health: Zoetis, Elanco
  • CDMO / CRO:Lonza, Samsung Biologics, WuXi AppTec
  • Mainly consumer health: Haleon, Kenvue, Perrigo, Yunnan Baiyao, CR Sanjiu
  • Mainly devices or infusion: Baxter, Fresenius
  • Royalty aggregators: Royalty Pharma
  • Mainly aesthetics: Galderma

Data grades

A The figure comes from the Pharma 50 table, or was verified against company announcements in this round.

B The figure comes from a secondary compilation and is consistent with the company's size, but has not been checked line by line against original announcements.

C Approximate. These companies are mostly private, need their business segments split, or only have data for older fiscal years. When the company's page is completed, the figure is re-verified against the annual report.

Relative positions between #51 and #100 may move up or down by a few places. The revenue threshold after #90 is about $1.0–1.6B, so companies at the margin may later be replaced.

Corporate status to watch

  • Organon: Sun Pharma is acquiring Organon at an enterprise value of $11.75B; Organon shareholders approved it in 2026-07, and closing is expected in early 2027. Organon remains a standalone node for now; after closing it becomes a Sun Pharma subsidiary node, and existing collaboration records keep the original name.
  • AstraZeneca × BMS: in 2026-08 media reported that the two had discussed a merger; neither confirmed. For now they are treated as two independent companies.
  • Tanabe Pharma: formerly Mitsubishi Tanabe; spun off from Mitsubishi Chemical in 2025 and acquired by Bain Capital, renamed in 2025-12.
  • Chugai: Roche owns about 60%, but Chugai is separately listed. It appears as an independent node in the graph, connected to Roche with an ownership line.
  • BeOne Medicines: formerly BeiGene; renamed in 2025 and moved its headquarters to Basel. Its region is classified as China by place of founding.

Supporting nodes

Historically important companies that have been acquired or are smaller are not ranked; they enter the collaboration graph as small nodes, connected to their current parent by an ownership line. Examples: Genentech (into Roche, 2009), Celgene (into BMS, 2019), Wyeth and Schering-Plough (into Pfizer and Merck respectively, 2009), Allergan (into AbbVie, 2020), Shire (into Takeda, 2019), Alexion (into AstraZeneca, 2021), Seagen (into Pfizer, 2023), Kite (into Gilead, 2017). Key partners such as Akeso, Summit, Halozyme and Legend appear in the same way.

Products and loss of exclusivity

Phase 3 added profiles (founding year, headquarters, description, areas of strength) and FY2025 top-five products for the prototype companies. Product revenue comes from company FY2025 results announcements, 10-K / 20-F filings, and the FY2025 best-selling drug tables compiled by Drug Discovery & Development from primary documents. The denominator for revenue share is the company's drug revenue; Merck and Novartis use the drug sales basis the companies disclose themselves, as stated on the card.

LOE (loss of exclusivity) is listed line by line by region and basis, for example US 2028 core patent expiry, EU patent plus SPC, and the generic launch date set by a patent settlement. A specific year is written only when the company discloses it explicitly or it is widely reported; otherwise it is marked "to be verified" (待核实), to be checked against 10-K patent tables in Phase 4. Revenue figures that cannot be verified are shown as "FY2025 to be verified", not estimated.

Profiles and product data for all 16 prototype companies have been added. Non-USD figures are converted at IRS 2025 average exchange rates (EUR 0.886, GBP 0.759, DKK 6.617 per US dollar), consistent with the Top 100 ranking basis, and the cards keep the original-currency amounts. Novo Nordisk's insulins and Gilead's liver disease and cell therapy are listed as product groups; Regeneron lists its own revenue basis, with Dupixent counted as collaboration revenue. Daiichi Sankyo's fiscal year ends March 2026, and its per-product figures have not yet been verified.

Historical Top 100 (Phase 9)

With Historical Top 100 on, the time slider also decides which ranking snapshot is shown: 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020, 2025, taking the nearest one not later than the selected year. Only companies verifiable in that snapshot's source appear; companies that dropped off the list or are not covered by the source disappear from the graph. Acquired historical companies (Aventis, Pharmacia, Wyeth, Schering-Plough) appear as normal solid nodes in the years when they were still independent and listed, with node size based on that year's revenue. Collaboration lines show only collaborations where both ends are on the list that year and the signing year is not later than the selected year. Roll-up is switched off automatically in this mode.

Limitations: public historical rankings mostly stop at the top 20–50 and use inconsistent bases. c.2000 uses 2002 pharma revenue (verified only to the top 8), 2005 uses Fortune's top 12 pharmaceutical companies by total revenue, 2010 uses IMS's top 20 by prescription drug sales, and 2015 and 2020 use about 25 still-independent companies from a Wikipedia revenue table (2016 data stand in for 2015). Earlier snapshots therefore show only the leading companies; companies ranked 21–100 are hidden because they cannot be verified, not filled in by guesswork. Click See ranking in the snapshot note to view the full list and sources of each snapshot.

The current ranking (2025) has been re-sorted using revenue verified or corrected against annual reports in each batch of this round, so some companies' positions differ from the first version (for example, Pierre Fabre moved down markedly after being changed to count prescription drugs only). The list itself has not been re-screened, and whether any company outside No. 100 should be included is still to be verified.

Compare and competition

The Compare page is ordered by priority: first the collaborations between the two companies (including those signed through acquired companies), then company profile, top five products, next US LOE, recent events and 5-year return, and only last competition.

Competition relationships are kept in a separate database and are recorded only where there is a specific product or indication overlap, marked Current (marketed products in head-to-head competition) or Emerging (at least one side still in clinical development). Being in the same broad area (for example both in oncology) does not count as competition. If no competition relationship is recorded for two companies, the Compare page says so directly instead of forcing an analysis. The competitor layer in the graph is off by default, and when turned on it shows as orange dashed lines. 29 competition groups are currently recorded, concentrated among the 16 prototype companies.

Recent developments and stock data

Each prototype company has 3–4 recent events recorded, chosen only for what changes the understanding of the company: major approvals, clinical successes or failures, major deals and acquisitions, loss of exclusivity, strategic shifts. It is not a news feed. Entries with a Source link have been verified against the original text.

The share-price charts for the 16 prototype companies use monthly prices from Digrin (full page), taking the closing price at the end of each quarter. Companies in the extended batches use only the monthly data visible in search results, so their charts start in 2022 (about 4 years) and the card heading states the starting quarter; the charts use monthly prices from Digrin, taking the closing price at the end of each quarter, adjusted for dividends and splits, so the 5-year return approximates total return. To compare in one currency, non-US companies use US-listed ADRs or OTC quotes (USD), and exchange-rate movements are included in the return. A few exceptions: the GSK series breaks because of the 2022-07 Haleon spin-off and share consolidation, so its chart starts in 2022-09; the Novartis series does not include the share distribution from the 2023-10 Sandoz spin-off, so its return is understated by about 5 percentage points. Data are a snapshot and are not updated in real time.

Collaboration data

Phase 2 recorded about 95 collaborations of the 16 prototype companies, spanning 1977–2026, and also covers their partners (including other Top 100 companies and acquired historical companies). The inclusion criterion is drug-related collaboration: licensing, co-development, co-commercialization, research collaboration, asset acquisition and joint ventures. Operational contracts such as pure supply or contract manufacturing are not included.

Each card shows the signing year. For a few collaborations the signing year has not been verified; the card says "approval year" and sorts by approval year. Status falls into four categories: Active (ongoing), Product marketed, deal historical, Ended, and Failed or terminated.

Sources are given as "publisher + date". Cards for which the original text was found carry an Open ↗ link; the others provide Find the original announcement ↗, which searches directly by drug name and both company names so you can check for yourself. Source links for product data are listed at the end of each company's product list.

Schema

Data structure design. Core principle: each collaboration is an independent record, and a line on the graph is only a summary of those records.

Rules

Deals attach to the entity that signed them.
Humulin is attached between Genentech ↔ Eli Lilly: Genentech supplied the recombinant DNA technology, Lilly handled development scale-up and commercialization, and it was approved in 1982. Roche is linked to Genentech by the 2009 ownership line, but is not written as a party to Humulin.
One edge, many deals.
Multiple collaborations between the same pair of companies are kept as separate deal cards, ordered by year. Line thickness reflects the number of collaborations, and color reflects the status of the most recent one.
Roll-up is a view setting, not a data change.
With "roll up to current parent" on, Genentech is shown merged into Roche, but the deal card still says Genentech and notes its acquisition by Roche in 2009.
Loss of exclusivity is stored per region and basis.
LOE is recorded in separate rows by region (US / EU / Japan / China) and basis (compound patent, regulatory exclusivity, settlement, biosimilar entry), and is not merged into a single date.
Missing beats invented.
Unknown fields show "—" and are marked as to be verified in Source.

How to read this

A map of who partners with whom, how big each company is, and where its revenue is exposed.

1. Read the graph

  • Node = company; size = FY2025 prescription drug revenue. Hollow small nodes are acquired or historical companies.
  • Line = drug collaboration (licensing, co-development, acquisition of an asset). Thicker = more deals; bright solid = at least one still active. Click a line to open every deal between the two.
  • Grey dashed = ownership; orange dashed = competition (switch on "Competitor layer").

2. Click a company

  • Top five products and their share of revenue show concentration: a company living off one drug is riskier than one with many.
  • LOE chart (loss of exclusivity) shows when generics or biosimilars may arrive: the "patent cliff". Light dots have already happened.
  • Recent events and a 5-year share price give context, but the price is a quarter-end snapshot in local currency or ADR.

3. Questions it helps with

  • Who depends on one blockbuster, and when does it lose protection?
  • Which big companies buy or license innovation, and from whom (for example Chinese biotechs)?
  • Who competes head-to-head on the same product or indication?
  • Use Compare to put two companies side by side; use the time slider and Historical Top 100 to see how the league table changed since 2000.

4. What to be careful about

  • Grade A/B/C shows how well a revenue figure is verified. "待核实" / "to be verified" means unverified; blanks are deliberate, never filled by guesswork.
  • Pipelines are summarised from company sources as of November 2026 (open a company and see Pipeline); LOE for several large companies is still being verified. It shows what is known about the past and the marketed portfolio, not future winners.
  • Compiled from public sources and may contain errors. Informational only, not investment advice.
